We audit eight open-weight LLMs to test whether name-coded Indian identity shifts their yes/no decisions on job, loan and scholarship applications, and whether their explanations or self-predictions reveal it. Using 150 synthetic applications in English and Hindi, paired name swaps and same-group controls, we find small shifts (|ΔP(Yes)| ≤ 2.16 pp), but female- and Northeast-coded names tilt flips towards Yes in 7 of 8 models. No explanation of an identity-flipped decision names the attribute (0 of 576), and self-predictions cannot be told apart from chance near the decision threshold. An explicit-label control shows the measures work: stated caste shifts P(Yes) by up to 9.72 pp and is named in some explanations. Caste-coded surnames show no effect on the primary test, and models answer "Cannot tell" when asked a name's caste, so this null reflects non-perception. Explanation review and self-report cannot replace paired behavioural audits.
